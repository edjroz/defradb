Range-based set reconciliation vs default sync

The case in one line: to discover a tiny difference inside a large set, the default sync must communicate the whole set (O(n) control bytes), while negentropy narrows in on just the difference (O(d·log n)) — a 188× control-traffic reduction at n=100k for a 2-item difference, at the cost of a few extra logarithmic round-trips.

Negentropy numbers are measured directly from the Phase-2 engine (internal/db/p2p/negentropy); the default-sync baseline is modelled as a naive full-identifier exchange at the same per-item accounting — a generous lower bound on what the real DAG-walk costs. See tests/bench/reconcile/README.md for methodology.

Charts 1–4 are the modelled synthetic sweep (the asymptotic case). Charts 5–6 are measured on real nodes for Phase 3/M1: at per-document scale reconciliation costs more control traffic than broadcast doc-sync (one session per document), with identical payload. Charts 7–8 show M2's per-collection batching collapsing that per-document overhead back to ~broadcast levels. Chart 9 is the payoff measured on real nodes: with one changed document in a growing collection, default doc-sync's control traffic rises with the collection size (2.1KB→8.4KB from 50 to 200 docs) while reconciliation stays roughly flat (2.7KB→4.5KB) and crosses below it — the O(diff) win, not just modelled.
